What it tests

The hypothesis

By removing traditional threat-based failure states and replacing them with curiosity-driven retention loops and an interlocking harvest economy, a game can sustain long-term engagement purely through intrinsic motivation and emergent ecosystem payoffs.

The core questions

  • How to create a cozy progressive experience, and what systems feel well in this context?
  • How much player agency is needed when there is no threat?
  • What specific actions are important to hold attention and drive the progression curve?

Curiosity as the retention loop

To hold attention without a fail state, the UX hierarchy must clearly signpost the unknown, driving human-centered focus toward discovery rather than survival.

The Unfinished Goal: Relying on locked seeds, undiscovered hybrids, and empty ponds fuels intrinsic motivation. The player returns not because they are punished for leaving, but because the system flowcharts promise a satisfying resolution to a missing piece of the puzzle.

Harvest as a Single Currency: Distilling progress into a single currency keeps the cognitive load light. It makes the core action loop—plant, wait, harvest, hybridize—feel effortlessly fluid, ensuring the progression curve never feels like a spreadsheet.

Agency through ecosystem payoffs

Without combat or physical pressure, player agency is entirely defined by environmental control and the resulting sensory feedback.

Wildlife as Visual Feedback: Having the ecosystem respond dynamically to specific plant combinations and weather acts as a living reward system. The meadow visibly evolving into a thriving habitat proves to the player that their specific choices matter, even without a win state.

Atmospheric Cohesion: Because the mechanical actions are gentle, the audiovisual feedback must carry the emotional weight. Pairing a distinct, minimalist visual style—perhaps utilizing clear, deliberate lines and surreal, vibrant landscapes—with a purely instrumental, atmospheric soundscape ensures the space feels deeply immersive and relaxing.

Growth speeds are deliberately slow — the test is whether that reads as calm or as dead time. Hybrids are currently discovered by adjacency; a recipe book would be clearer but removes the surprise. And the unlock gates are flat harvest counts, which is easy to tune but tells the player nothing about what they're building toward.

Systems in the build

~/prototypes/cozy-meadow
Growth & care
  • Till, sow, water, harvest with tool-based input
  • Per-plant growth rates, from grass to oak
Progression
  • Harvest-count gates unlock new species
  • Unlocked seeds are granted, not bought — no wall
Hybrids
  • Crossbreeds from parent pairs — rose + daisy, sunflower + bluebell, dandelion + tulip
  • Encyclopedia hides undiscovered hybrids as mysteries
Habitat & weather
  • Bees, butterflies, birds and frogs with their own behaviour
  • Weather changes flight, altitude and where fauna shelter
  • Ponds and rocks as placeable terrain
