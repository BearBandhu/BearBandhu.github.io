Cusco
Paradox

Cusco Paradox is an LGBTQ+ narrative puzzle game about a fox being trapped in the Incan netherworld. A journey of forlorn love and discovery, Atoq the Fox has lost his memories and must delve deep into this world to recover both his memories and his boyfriend. Move the blocks to build a path through the shrines and travel the nether.

Team & pipeline

4 people, one month

My daily pipeline is heavily structured around alignment, iterative momentum, and bridging the gap of a fully remote team. To maintain the frictionless collaboration of a physical studio, I implemented a 'virtual office' approach using Discord, where every team member maintained their own active voice channel.

We kick off with a morning sync to prioritize tasks, and throughout the day, this open-channel setup allows us to drop in and resolve blockers instantly without scheduling overhead. In the afternoons, I pair up with my co-designer to review work and iterate. We close out with a brief end-of-day touchpoint to catch any deviations, allowing me to prep solutions for the next morning.

To anchor this highly active environment, I enforce strict meeting hygiene: I conclude every discussion by verbally summarizing our agreements and immediately post the actionable notes to our group chat for complete visibility.

Postmortem
Process & Pipeline: Our team retrospective prioritized optimizing cross-disciplinary communication, specifically streamlining the handover documentation for system designs between the design and programming teams.
Gameplay & Onboarding: Playtesting validated our puzzle design, revealing a successful, dynamic difficulty curve that accommodated various player skill levels. However, it also highlighted a clear need to iterate on the tutorial to create a more frictionless onboarding experience.
Scalability & Vision: Following the project’s delivery, client strategy discussions shifted toward the game's future architecture—focusing on how to evolve the current linear narrative structure into a highly scalable, procedural system.
