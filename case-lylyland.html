Market research · gamified loyalty apps, 2024

Competitor case studiesTemu · McDonald's · Chipotle · Fetch
Cross-border e-commerce

Fishland, Farmland · resource farming, social referrals

How it works
Users pick a free physical item, then water crops or feed fish to earn it. Water and food come from daily logins, browsing categories for 45–60 seconds, sharing links and referring new users.
What worked
Endowed progress: items hit 80–90% completion in days, creating sunk cost. Forced referrals pushed acquisition costs to historic lows.
What failed
Rewards throttle exponentially near 99%. Items worth about $30 took 8–10 months of daily logins. Loyalty fatigue, brand damage and mercenary users who never made organic purchases.
Outcome
Massive DAU, high fatigue, dark-pattern backlash
Quick-service restaurant

MyMcDonald's Rewards · tiered milestones, rolling expiry

How it works
100 points per dollar, visible reward tiers from 1,500 to 7,000 points, live progress bars and time-limited challenges like breakfast three days in a row.
What worked
Progress bars triggered the goal-gradient effect, pushing impulse add-ons. Points expire after six months, driving routine earn-and-burn. Visits rose from 10.5 to 26 a year.
What failed
Engagement spikes overwhelmed drive-thrus and kitchens. Little reason to open the app when not hungry.
Outcome
175M+ active users · ~150% visit frequency
Fast casual dining

Chipotle IQ · time-gated trivia, badges, sweepstakes

How it works
10 points per dollar, plus "Extras" challenges with collectible badges. Chipotle IQ is a recurring brand trivia game with rewards up to free burritos for a year.
What worked
Brand education as entertainment. Time windows and capped winners drove simultaneous logins: 1.6M completed quizzes, up 24% year over year.
What failed
Episodic: DAU spikes vanished after each campaign. Answer keys spread on Reddit, so rewards went to mercenary users.
Outcome
1.6M engagements per event
Universal loyalty, receipt scanning

adjoe Arcade · third-party play-to-earn

How it works
Users play top third-party mobile games inside Fetch and earn points for playtime, redeemable as gift cards.
What worked
Outsourced game development. 10B+ points earned ($25M in rewards), +10% LTV, +5% engagement and 5%+ monthly growth in app opens, without cannibalising receipt scanning.
What failed
Margins depend on volatile ad budgets after Apple's App Tracking Transparency. Attracts users grinding playtime, not engaging with brand partners.
Outcome
+10% LTV · $25M reward value

Shopee

Shopee Farm and Shopee Candy. 2024 research found a moderate positive effect on brand loyalty and repeat purchases in Indonesia.

Starbucks

Seasonal sweepstakes games: 68% of brand social discussion referenced the launch, across 34.3M rewards members. Custom builds need budgets mid-market brands can't match.

Adding a game does not make an app loyal by itself. It amplifies the architecture underneath: extractive designs burn users out faster, and user-centric designs build habits faster.

Strategic insights3 takeaways
01

Evergreen over episodic

Campaigns like Chipotle IQ spike DAU. Long-term churn reduction comes from permanent mechanics like rolling points and daily streaks.

02

Experiential rewards protect margins

Badges, status tiers and early access drive behaviour at zero marginal cost, where discounts and free items erode margin.

03

Games collect zero-party data

Users who ignore surveys share preferences through trivia and challenges, feeding segmentation and personalisation.

Key findings

Casual beats long

Engagement was strongest around casual games. Games that needed longer play sessions had lower retention.

Prizes bring players back

Rewarding players with prizes kept them returning to the app.

Competition retains

Retention increased on competitive games.

Quests near the daily route

Users engaged with daily quests set around places near their usual foot traffic.

Three systems, one loop
Retention loops & meta-progressionChests · discounts · raffles

Every game feeds one shared economy. In-game objectives earn chests, and chests unlock discounts at real partner stores. Seasonal raffles sit on top, giving players a prize to keep coming back for.

Real-world questsTwo quest types

Narrative quests send users to real places to take a photo or read the space for clues to a riddle. Daily quests were placed near the places users already pass through.

Active behaviour

Quests that ask the user to do something on site, such as saying something to staff.

Consumption

Quests built around consuming a product at the location.

Brand partnerships & monetisationSponsors · ads · discounts

Sponsored games

In-game placements

Partner discounts

Rewards paid out as discounts at the partner's own stores, so each prize brings a customer through the door.

Roadmap · what was missing when I left

A central meta-progression loop

Players had points and leaderboards inside each game, but the app had no central meta-progression around avatars and personalisation. The next step on the roadmap was digital rewards that users earn by using the app and by consuming products at partner franchises.

That way, using the app and engaging with the franchises would both have in-game results, and those rewards cost less to offer than discounts.

Before

Siloed progress

Points and leaderboards inside each game. Discounts as the only shared reward.

Planned

Avatars & app personalisation

One progression across the app, fed by play and by purchases at partner locations. Digital rewards at near-zero marginal cost.

Postmortem

Retention vs churn

The games captured short-term engagement well but needed deeper progression to hold veteran users.

LiveOps upkeep

Content-driven games like La Trivia needed ongoing maintenance, which took a lot of a small team's bandwidth.

Interconnectivity

The games worked too independently. They needed stronger links to each other and to the wider app.
