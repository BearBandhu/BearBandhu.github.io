# 01 — operating principles

How I think about making people stay.

01 / RETENTION
Retention is a system, never a feature.
Sticky products earn each return with overlapping loops — habit cues, perceived progress, social texture, content velocity. I map the loops before drawing pixels. Game designers have been doing this for decades; product hasn't caught up.

Step 1: flatten the funnel into a loop. What event has to repeat? What triggers it? What blocks it from repeating?

Step 2: measure the gap between trigger and reward. Shorter is better, but predictable is the actual metric.

Step 3: layer a second loop — usually social, content, or status — to catch users who fall out of the first.

02 / INFORMATION
Make finding feel like remembering.
Navigation should match how someone thinks about their own work. Good IA lets users shortcut their own future selves. I design the search before I design the screens. This site uses the principle on itself — try ⌘K.

Every dense product has at least two readers: the scanner (who arrived with a question) and the explorer (who arrived with time). Same screen, different jobs.

I design two reading layers from the start — a TL;DR for the scanner, progressive disclosure for the explorer. (Toggle DEEP/SKIM in the top bar.)

03 / SYSTEMS
Components compound; screens decay.
I lead with patterns and pull tokens behind. A clean component library is worth more than a beautiful screen — particularly in the kind of fast-iteration startup environments I've spent the last five years in.
04 / METHOD
Prototype first. Then argue.
A working prototype kills a hundred Slack threads. I push teams toward clickable artifacts inside the first week — even if it embarrasses everyone, including me. Mostly because of my time in game design, where you have to feel a system to know if it works.
