Product design · Bolea · Peru · sports matchmaking app

Bolea

A social app for finding teams and matches for sports like padel. In Peru there is no Playtomic-style app, so players organise through WhatsApp groups of thousands of people. I led product design: the system, its logic, the screen flow, the UI and the brand book.

01

Problem

WhatsApp as a matchmaking tool

Player pain points

  • People want to find teams and matches, but WhatsApp is still the tool of choice over current apps.
  • Most players use WhatsApp groups made up of thousands of people, where teams and matches are posted.
  • Readability is terrible: posts are buried in a constant stream of messages.
  • Finding a group means scrolling through huge lists, then copy-pasting the list to sign up.

Business goals

Bolea earns through memberships for two audiences.

  • Players: a membership automates the search for matches and personalises the matches they're offered.
  • Organisers: a membership gives them more tools to find players for their events, plus branding inside the app: images, videos and fully branded profiles.
02

Discovery

Research, competitors, insights

Research summary

Method

Interviews, surveys, shadowing and A/B testing with players who use booking apps and players who coordinate on WhatsApp. We also interviewed court owners.

Participants

Amateur padel and football players in Lima, from weekly regulars with a fixed group to solo players looking for a match through large WhatsApp groups, plus court and club owners.

Key finding

Players didn't want another app. WhatsApp felt good enough because it was familiar and everyone was already there. Players with a set group of friends didn't use apps at all.

People

Affinity mapPain points & motivations · 4 pillars

Research notes clustered into four pillars, ordered the way Bolea answers them: stay inside WhatsApp, find people, organise the game, then open it up to organisers and courts. The same patterns showed up across padel, football and other sports and activities.

01

Staying in WhatsApp

“Everyone is already in the group. Nobody is going to download another app.”

“My friends would never make an account just to play on Thursday.”

“I tried an app once and ended up sharing the link in WhatsApp anyway.”

Core challenge

WhatsApp is familiar and everyone is there, so new apps feel unnecessary.

Bolea answer

A link shared in the chat opens Bolea inside WhatsApp and logs in with WhatsApp. No download, no new account.

02

Finding players

“I want to play, but my usual group isn’t free tonight.”

“I post in five groups and the message gets buried.”

“We’re always one person short.”

Core challenge

Groups depend on who you already know, and requests get lost in busy chats.

Bolea answer

Players post when they want to play or join open lobbies. Owners can invite people. Next: Bolea looks for players for you.

03

Organising the game

“Half an hour of messages just to agree on a time and a place.”

“Someone always drops out at the last minute.”

“Nobody reads the pinned message.”

Core challenge

Dates, places and who’s in are scattered across one long chat.

Bolea answer

Lobbies with a set date and location, their own chat, and every notification delivered to a WhatsApp conversation.

04

Organisers & courts

“Filling the last tournament spots takes longer than running it.”

“We have empty courts every weekday afternoon.”

“Booking platforms don’t leave room for our brand.”

Core challenge

Organisers and courts can only reach their own contacts and have no branded presence.

Bolea answer

Organisations post tournaments and lobbies to fill courts, and customise lobby visuals with their branding. Roadmap: court booking and payment.

Competitor analysis5 products in the Lima market
01

Playmix

Social matchmaking and progression platform.

Core mechanics
Utilizes game design principles. The primary loop consists of playing a match, inputting the score, and adjusting an internal skill ranking. It includes meta-game elements like achievements, peer rating systems, and performance tracking.
UX focus
Player profiles, community leaderboards, and skill-based matching interfaces.
Strengths
High user retention driven by progression systems and social graphs.
Weaknesses
Susceptible to the cold start problem. Matchmaking algorithms require a dense, localized concurrent player base to function effectively.
02

ATC (Alquila Tu Cancha)

E-commerce marketplace.

Core mechanics
Search, filter by availability or location, and complete checkout.
UX focus
Transaction optimization. The user flow prioritizes search-to-booking conversion, similar to digital travel booking platforms.
Strengths
High liquidity of venues. It maintains established B2B integrations with sports complexes across Latin America.
Weaknesses
Strictly transactional. It lacks retention mechanics or progression systems, providing users no incentive to engage with the product outside of the booking action.
03

Quipo

Localized amateur sports network.

Core mechanics
Real-time slot visibility and group coordination specific to the Lima market.
UX focus
Reducing user input required to find available playing partners and coordinate group matches.
Strengths
Solves the specific coordination friction of filling a roster (e.g., finding a fourth player) within a defined geographic area.
Weaknesses
Feature set is highly replicable by larger platforms possessing more engineering resources.
04

Pádel Lima

B2B2C White-label SaaS.

Core mechanics
Club administrators control back-office inventory; players interact with a centralized booking interface governed by those backend settings.
UX focus
Functional calendar views prioritizing club branding and operational management.
Strengths
Inventory lock-in. Venues relying on the software for operations dictate player adoption.
Weaknesses
The UX serves the facility operator before the end user, often resulting in fragmented player experiences across different clubs.
05

ReservaSimple

General appointment and scheduling SaaS.

Core mechanics
Web-link access, automated WhatsApp reminders, and integrated local payment gateways.
UX focus
Web-based transaction flows. It bypasses native app downloads entirely.
Strengths
Eliminates app store acquisition friction. The business model charges venues a fixed subscription rather than per-transaction commissions, incentivizing venues to direct their user traffic to the platform.
Weaknesses
Operates as a utility link rather than a destination. It lacks player discovery, social graphs, and progression loops.

Strategic product gaps (opportunities)

Utility vs. Retention

Current platforms segment into pure utilities (ATC, ReservaSimple) or early-stage social networks (Playmix). A product bridging this gap by applying live-ops retention mechanics—giving users reasons to open the application daily for meta-game progression, localized leaderboards, or rewards—can lower churn rates compared to transaction-only platforms.

Onboarding Architecture

Forcing a native app download for a first-time booking creates a high drop-off rate. Utilizing a Progressive Web App (PWA) or web-first flow for initial conversion, followed by an app download prompt gated behind progression incentives, optimizes Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC).

The Lifecycle Loop

Competing on venue acquisition is a standard sales cycle. Competing on player lifecycle—building features that guide beginners through skill levels and match them accurately—generates a localized network effect. If the product controls the player base through these mechanics, venue adoption becomes mandatory.
